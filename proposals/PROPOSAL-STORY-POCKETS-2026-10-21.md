# Story pockets — what the locality review means for vugg, and the work it calls for (2026-10-21)

**Status:** proposal. Nothing in the simulator is changed. It builds on
`REVIEW-LOCALITY-ABUNDANCE-2026-10-21.md` (the review, its data, its instruments and six review
rounds) and replaces that review's fix order (§8 there) with one ranked for the purpose the boss
stated after round 6.
**New instrument:** `tools/showcase-scoreboard.mjs` — passive; it reads the committed census
(861 runs, 21 seeds per scenario) and the comparison's output, and runs no new simulation.
**Numbers:** every table below is generated by that tool or from its output; none is typed by hand.

---

## 1. The purpose

The boss, 2026-10-21:

> "if we are going totally by the science there are likely many pockets where nothing of note ever
> really happened, vugg simulator is more interested in the pockets where something happened, a story
> worth sharing. there will be collector bias for the data we are pulling from these sources, but more
> than accurate ratios it will tell us what minerals people want to see from those locations"

> "both of those things can be true at once. and there are other reasons why a mineral might be
> underrepresented. it might not be abundant, it might not be collector grade. for example no collector
> is going to care about the locality information of some vein fill quartz unless it also has gold in it."

A locality scenario models a **story pocket**: a real pocket of that deposit, chosen because something
worth showing happened in it, not the average pocket. That changes what the review's three sources
are for.

| source | what it records | what it cannot see |
|---|---|---|
| literature (63 sources, 278 classes) | which species occur in the deposit, and roughly how abundant they are | collector grade — it lists vein quartz and gold alike |
| photographs (Mineralienatlas, 6,736) | what collectors found worth photographing: abundance × collector grade × desire, with a rarity premium (type localities, micromount rarities) | anonymous gangue; the average pocket |
| listings (eBay, 15,190) | what sells from the place — collector-grade material people buy, often as an association ("gold in quartz") | species nobody names in a title |

The record — photographs and listings — is a biased stand-in for abundance and a fair measure of desire
at the same time. A species can be thin in it because it is rare, because it is not collector grade
(massive, fine-grained, dull, a crust), or because its locality adds nothing (vein quartz without gold).
It can be thick because it is showy, because it is rare and famous, or because it rides on a classic
association.

### 1.1 The story-pocket contract (proposed)

1. **Possible.** Everything a scenario grows must occur in the part of the deposit it models (the
   literature and the chemistry). A species its sources place only elsewhere stays out — the review's
   scope rule, a presence test.
2. **Showcase.** The pocket's most visible crystals — the largest, the ones a collector photographs
   first — are species people want from the place (the record), at a size worth showing.
3. **Supporting cast.** An abundant species the record ignores (vein quartz, a matrix carbonate, a
   crust) belongs in the pocket, but not as its star.
4. **The occasional showpiece.** A rarity the record loves may appear in some seeds, where the sources
   place it in the modelled pocket.
5. **Clutter.** A visible species with no photograph, no listing and no literature class is first a
   science question: is it there at all?

### 1.2 What this changes in the review

- **Ratios become a diagnostic, not the target.** The review's headline — no scenario matches its
  record's ratios (median ρ −0.04) — stands as a measurement. But a story pocket can hold one large
  calcite and fifty grains of something else and still be right.
- **Size moves to the top.** A signature species grown as dust (the review's F3) is the worst failure:
  the story has no protagonist.
- **The count lever drops.** The strict nucleation gate (the review's F1b) matters less when the
  showcase is one big crystal: Tri-State's calcite has a median largest crystal of 38 mm and holds 94 %
  of the crystal volume.
- **The wrong star and visible clutter move up** (the review's F2 and its OVER flags).
- **The review's trap (a) is refined.** "Calibrate to the photograph shares" stays a trap for crystal
  counts — one showcase crystal can carry half of a locality's photographs — but the record does say
  which species to feature, and how prominently.
- **The contract question in the review's §4.1(a) gets an answer.** Claim a never-forming species where
  it is a signature of the place and its sources put it in the modelled pocket.

## 2. Where the scenarios stand: the showcase scoreboard

Per scenario the tool takes:
- **the signature species:** the record's top three; for the three scenarios without a voting record,
  the collectible their sources name;
- **each one's largest crystal per seed** that is not flagged as enclosed: ≥ 10 mm is "cabinet",
  ≥ 5 mm "thumbnail", ≥ 1 mm "visible", smaller "dust";
- **the star:** the species of the largest crystal in a seed;
- **the signature species' share** of the visible volume;
- **the visible clutter.**

Most vugs are about 50 mm across (26–226 mm), so a 10 mm crystal dominates a typical pocket. The status
is one of three:
- **SHOWCASE:** every signature species with an engine reaches 5 mm in at least half the seeds, and the
  star is a signature species in at least half.
- **PARTIAL:** at least one signature species reaches 1 mm in half the seeds.
- **MISSING:** otherwise.

**Why first.** These are the stories the scenarios exist to tell. Herkimer's quartz is 94 % of its
record ("Herkimer diamonds"), Cruzeiro's tourmaline 83 % and Ametista's amethyst 56 %.

**The science.** The sources say the crystals are large where the story is:
- "Pockets in this region frequently contain superb gem tourmaline crystals" at Cruzeiro (Proctor 1985).
- Large amethyst lines the Ametista geodes (Proust & Fontaine 2007).
- Selenite grows to 11 m at Naica (García-Ruiz et al. 2007).
- Chiastolitic andalusite reaches 14 cm at Bimbowrie (Teale 1979).
- Calcite scalenohedra reach 2–3 ft at Picher (Ransome 1935).

In nature, crystals grow large where few nuclei share a supply for a long time, close to equilibrium,
and Naica's are the extreme case. In the simulator, dust means one of three things in a given
scenario:
- the species' growth window is too short;
- its supersaturation stays near the nucleation threshold;
- too many nuclei share the supply.

**The lever.** Diagnose each scenario's growth window first: the species' σ history, the event that
feeds it, and its number of nuclei. Then change the scenario (broth, event timing) before any engine
rate. Quartz grows in 19 scenarios, so a global quartz rate would move all of them.

**Target (pre-register before any change).** Every signature species with an engine reaches 5 mm in at
least half the seeds, and the scenario's star is a wanted species in at least half.

**Cases that need a decision first.**
- **Naica.** The scenario declares "the sim isn't trying to reproduce Naica scale, it's reproducing
  Naica chemistry", in a 50 mm vug. Its selenite is one crystal of 2.8 mm, outnumbered by six celestine
  crystals of 2.2 mm. The story of Naica is the giant crystal. Should the selenite dominate the pocket
  at the pocket's scale, with celestine as the wall crust García-Ruiz et al. 2007 describe?
- **Habit, not size.**
  - Tiger's eye and crocidolite at the Asbestos Hills are fibrous seams, not single crystals, so their
    showcase measure is the seam.
  - Schneeberg's erythrite is a micromount species in a micromount-heavy photograph archive, so
    "visible" may be its honest target.
- **Signature species that never form.** These are Shigar's aquamarine, Mogok's ruby, Sicily's
  aragonite, Viburnum's calcite and chalcopyrite, Tsumeb's and Ametista's calcite, Cripple Creek's
  fluorite, Ouro Preto's hematite, Wittichen's zeunerite, the reactivated vein's quartz, Marlborough's
  chalcedony and Grimsel's rutile. Each is first the review's contract question (§4.1(a) there), which
  this purpose answers: claim the species where it is a signature and its sources put it in the pocket.
  Then comes a diagnosis — σ gate, broth or missing event.
  - The July rule stands: never edit `expects_species` to accept a no-show.
  - Two already carry their own words: aquamarine is "the documented Shigar showpiece" and ruby "the
    documented Mogok target"; both are listed as aspirational.
  - The sources for the rest are in the data file — for example, "die bekannten grossen
    Aragonitkrystalle" in Sicily (Viola 1897), and chalcopyrite as "the main source of byproduct
    copper" in the Viburnum Trend (Leach et al. 2010).

**The lever.** Mostly WP1: a protagonist that grows to size outranks the cast. Where the cast still

**Target.** The star is a wanted species in at least half the seeds.

### WP4 — Decide the enclosure rule (as in the review)

The enclosure flag hides two signature species: Tsumeb's smithsonite and Bingham's pyrite reach 2–3 mm
but are flagged as enclosed by molybdate crusts up to 34 mm away. The cap rule then lets flagged
species nucleate again: 55 (scenario, species) pairs pass their caps (the review's §4.4 and §8 step 4).

For story pockets the first effect matters most: a hidden protagonist is a lost story. The decision is
the boss's — how far a crystal's footprint may reach, and whether a footprint-flagged crystal frees a
cap slot.

### WP6 — The occasional showpiece (a design question)

The record's rarity premium suggests that a story pocket sometimes holds a rarity: Tsumeb's ludlockite
out-photographs its anglesite, and Schneeberg's and Wittichen's archives are micromount photography of
rare arsenates. The scenarios already have a statistical tier, for species delivered in some seeds.

**Proposal:** allow a rare showpiece in that tier where the sources place it in the modelled pocket, and
measure how often it appears. Nothing changes until the boss decides.

### WP7 — Later: the count lever

The review's strict-gate replacement is demoted, since one large crystal can be the whole story. Revisit
it scenario by scenario after WP1, wherever a scenario's story is a druse or a cluster rather than a
single crystal.

## 4. Proposed order, and the boss's decisions

1. **Decisions (no code):**
   - the story-pocket contract (§1.1);
   - Naica's scale;
   - the enclosure rule (WP4);
   - the rarity tier (WP6);
   - per scenario, whether to claim each never-forming signature species (WP1).
2. **Pre-register the targets** in a side file the scoreboard reads: per scenario, the signature
   species, their size class and the expected star. This is the review's §8 step 1, reframed. It costs
   nothing, because the targets do not go in `data/scenarios.json5`, which is hashed release content and
   bundled.
3. **WP4**, if the boss changes the enclosure rule. It moves counts and hides signature species, so it
   comes before any tuning.
4. **WP1, one scenario at a time, MISSING ones first:** Herkimer and Ametista, then Cruzeiro, Searles'
   halite, Bingham's pyrite and Schneeberg's erythrite. For each: probe the growth window, change the
   scenario, re-run the census, check the scoreboard.
5. **WP2 and WP3**, re-measured after WP1 (a grown protagonist fixes many stars).
6. **WP5 engines**, by demand.
7. **WP6 and WP7**, after the decisions.

Every step uses:
- the two-commit pattern: a byte-identical instrument first, then the SIM bump;
- a census re-run;
- the showcase scoreboard as the acceptance test, with the review's comparison kept as the diagnostic.

## 5. Limits

- **The record is a mixture.** It mixes abundance, collector grade, desire and market noise, so the
  scoreboard uses it to choose what to feature, never as a count target.
- **"Signature" is narrow.** It means the record's top three, which misses some species people want
  (Tsumeb's malachite and azurite). The "wanted" set — at least 5 % of the record — covers them for the
  star test.
- **Sizes are read against vugs of about 50 mm.** A Naica-scale or Tri-State-scale story needs either
  bigger vugs or a decision about scale.
- **The census is end-state.** It does not see a crystal that grew large and then dissolved.
- **Thin records make weak targets.** At Bingham, Sulphur Bank, Bimbowrie and the Great Salt Plains the
  literature carries the story.
