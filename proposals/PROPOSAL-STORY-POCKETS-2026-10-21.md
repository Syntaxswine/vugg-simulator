# Story pockets — what the locality review means for vugg, and the work it calls for (2026-10-21, revised 2026-10-22)

**Status:** proposal. Nothing in the simulator is changed. It builds on
`REVIEW-LOCALITY-ABUNDANCE-2026-10-21.md` (the review, its data, its instruments and six review
rounds) and replaces that review's fix order (§8 there) with one ranked for the purpose the boss
stated after round 6.

**Revised 2026-10-22.** The boss approved the direction and rejected its universal target — every
top-three signature species at 5 mm in at least half the seeds. This revision replaces that target
with targets pre-registered per scenario:
- each wanted species' role;
- its occurrence band, from the literature;
- its size target, from the collector record;
- the pocket's scale, against the cavity.

The scoreboard is rebuilt on them (§1.1, §1.3, §2). Its review rounds and the responses are in
`REVIEW-STORY-POCKETS-ROUNDS-2026-10-22.md`.

**Instruments.** All are passive: they read the committed census (861 runs, 21 seeds per scenario) and
committed data, and run no simulation.
- `tools/showcase-scoreboard.mjs` — the story-pocket scoreboard, rebuilt. It keeps the previous rule as
  its "before" column.
- `tools/story-pocket-targets.mjs` — writes and checks the pre-registered targets,
  `DATA-STORY-POCKET-TARGETS-2026-10-22.json` (its schema is `SCHEMA-STORY-POCKET-TARGETS.json`).
- `tools/story-pocket-size-evidence.mjs` — the photographed sizes, read from the caption capture
  `DATA-STORY-POCKET-SIZES-CAPTURE-2026-10-22.json`, whose extractor is
  `tools/mineralienatlas-size-capture.mjs`. Its audit — every crystal length used and every pair of
  photographs joined as one specimen — is `DATA-STORY-POCKET-SIZES-2026-10-22.md`.
- `tools/story-pocket-cavities.mjs` — each scenario's cavity, read from the constructed wall with no
  steps run.
- `tools/story-pocket-lib.mjs` — the shared pure functions, tested in
  `tests-js/story-pocket-scoreboard.test.ts`.
- `tools/substrate-census.mjs` (WP8) — where crystals start.

**Numbers.** Every table and tally of results below is generated by the tool that computes it and
spliced in between `<!-- generated:… -->` markers: the scoreboard writes the story-pocket results, and
the substrate census writes WP8's figures (`--splice <this file>`). Each tool's `--check-splice` confirms
that its blocks match a fresh run and that each block it owns appears exactly once. No result is typed
by hand: the prose points to the blocks rather than repeating their numbers, and the source counts in
§1's table are the review's (its data file). The comparison (`tools/locality-abundance-compare.mjs`) is
unchanged and stays the diagnostic.

## 1. The purpose

The boss, 2026-10-21:

> "if we are going totally by the science there are likely many pockets where nothing of note ever
> really happened, vugg simulator is more interested in the pockets where something happened, a story
> worth sharing. there will be collector bias for the data we are pulling from these sources, but more
> than accurate ratios it will tell us what minerals people want to see from those locations"

> "both of those things can be true at once. and there are other reasons why a mineral might be
> underrepresented. it might not be abundant, it might not be collector grade. for example no collector
> is going to care about the locality information of some vein fill quartz unless it also has gold in it."

And 2026-10-22, on the contract:

> "A locality scenario should generate a distribution of scientifically possible story pockets, not the
> same ideal assemblage every run. Signature minerals should be common, not guaranteed."

> "The market record tells us which mineral stories people value and their characteristic display
> scale. It does not require every signature mineral in every pocket. Pocket size, occurrence, and
> crystal size must remain distributions."

A locality scenario models **story pockets**: real pockets of that deposit, chosen because something
worth showing happened in them, not the average pocket. Run over many seeds, it should give a
distribution of them. That changes what the review's three sources are for.

| source | what it records | what it cannot see |
|---|---|---|
| literature (63 sources, 278 classes) | which species occur in the deposit, and roughly how abundant they are | collector grade — it lists vein quartz and gold alike |
| photographs (Mineralienatlas, 6,736) | what collectors found worth photographing: abundance × collector grade × desire, with a rarity premium (type localities, micromount rarities) — and, in the captions, at what size | anonymous gangue; the average pocket |
| listings (eBay, 15,190) | what sells from the place — collector-grade material people buy, often as an association ("gold in quartz") | species nobody names in a title |

The record — photographs and listings — is a biased stand-in for abundance and a fair measure of desire
at the same time. A species can be thin in it because it is rare, because it is not collector grade
(massive, fine-grained, dull, a crust), or because its locality adds nothing (vein quartz without gold).
It can be thick because it is showy, because it is rare and famous, or because it rides on a classic
association. So the record chooses the story and its scale; it never sets how often a species occurs.

### 1.1 The story-pocket contract (revised 2026-10-22)

1. **Possible.** Everything a scenario grows must occur in the part of the deposit it models (the
   literature and the chemistry). A species its sources place only elsewhere stays out — the review's
   scope rule, a presence test.
2. **A protagonist, not every signature.** A pocket tells its locality's story when one intended
   protagonist is there at the record's display scale: the primary species or an alternate (§1.3).
   A seed needs one recognizable protagonist, not all of the locality's signatures at once. The
   locality's top species need not share a pocket.
3. **Common, not guaranteed.** Each species with a role has a pre-registered occurrence band: the share
   of a scenario's pockets it should occur in. The band comes from the literature's abundance class,
   never from photograph or listing shares, which measure collector salience, not geological frequency.
   No band reaches 100 %: a species in every pocket is guaranteed, not common.
4. **Size from the collector record, judged in pockets that can hold it.**
   - A species' characteristic crystal is the median of its photographed crystal lengths, and the
     record's quartiles give its range. A run shows the species at the record's display scale when its
     largest crystal not flagged as enclosed lies between the record's lower quartile and its 90th
     percentile. Across a scenario's runs, the typical crystal — their median — is judged against the
     record's median: it meets the target within the record's middle half.
   - Size is judged only in runs where the species occurs, in a cavity long enough to hold the
     characteristic crystal.
   - A cavity too small for the primary's characteristic crystal is a **small pocket**. It may lack a
     showcase, and the locality's target is never shrunk to fit it. The fleet should also hold pockets
     large enough to tell the story at the record's scale.
   - Where the cavity cannot be classed — the primary's size is unknown, or only a specimen proxy longer
     than the cavity — a run without a story stays open: it may be a small pocket.
   - Specimen dimensions stand in for crystal lengths only as a labelled proxy. Where the photographs
     give too few of either, the size is unknown. There is no default size and no size floor.
5. **Supporting cast — usually present, because the mechanisms make it.** Real pockets usually carry
   minerals no collector names: Herkimer's dolomite rhombs are "the most common feature at every
   Herkimer deposit" (Hoisington). The rules don't require these minerals. They are there because of
   how the pocket formed, so a scenario should not list them by decree. The simulator should model the
   mechanisms that make them, and they should then turn up wherever those mechanisms run. The boss
   named two, both established geology (WP8):
   - a compatible surface to grow on;
   - earlier crystals that concentrated the fluid.

   Companions — the literature's abundant and common species that the record does not want — are held
   to their own bands. Wherever the supporting cast appears, it stays out of the star's place.
6. **The occasional showpiece.** A rarity the record loves, which the literature calls rare or very
   rare, is held to the rare band: present in some pockets, not most, and only where the sources place
   it in the modelled pocket.
7. **Clutter.** A visible species with no photograph, no listing and no literature class is first a
   science question: is it there at all?

### 1.2 What this changes in the review

- **Ratios become a diagnostic, not the target.** The review's headline — no scenario matches its
  record's ratios (median ρ −0.04) — stands as a measurement. But a story pocket can hold one large
  calcite and fifty grains of something else and still be right.
- **Size stays at the top, measured against the record.** A signature species grown as dust (the
  review's F3) is still the worst failure: the story has no protagonist. But the bar is now each
  species' own photographed scale, not one threshold for all. A crystal that passed the old 5 mm bar
  can fall well short of its record.
- **Occurrence becomes a band.** A protagonist that never forms is flagged against its band. It no
  longer sinks a scenario whose other protagonists tell the story.
- **The fleet should vary.** Pockets, occurrence and crystal size should each be a distribution across
  a scenario's seeds. Today they are not (§2.5, WP9).
- **The count lever drops.** The strict nucleation gate (the review's F1b) matters less when the
  showcase is one big crystal.
- **The wrong star and visible clutter move up** (the review's F2 and its OVER flags).
- **The supporting cast becomes a question about mechanisms, not a list.** Background minerals should
  come out of modelled processes (WP8), not out of species lists.
- **The review's trap (a) is refined.** "Calibrate to the photograph shares" stays a trap for crystal
  counts and for occurrence — one showcase crystal can carry half of a locality's photographs. But the
  record does say which species to feature, and at what size.
- **The contract question in the review's §4.1(a) gets an answer.** Claim a never-forming species where
  it is a signature of the place and its sources put it in the modelled pocket.

### 1.3 The targets (pre-registered; the rules await the boss)

`DATA-STORY-POCKET-TARGETS-2026-10-22.json` holds the targets the scoreboard reads, per locality
scenario and per species with a role. `tools/story-pocket-targets.mjs` generates it from four inputs:
- the record and the literature classes (the comparison's output; the scoreboard refuses a comparison
  whose hash differs from the one the targets were built from);
- the literature's sources;
- the size capture;
- each scenario's cavity span (`tools/story-pocket-cavities.mjs`: the authored wall, read before any
  run). The targets file records each span it used; the scoreboard refuses cavities that differ. It is
  checked by span rather than by hash, since the cavity file records its own run time.

Its `--check` mode confirms the committed file matches a rebuild. Nothing in it is fitted to the census:
the census is read only by the scoreboard. Each number in it is either evidence — a sample, with its
count and provenance — or one of the tool's stated rules.

**Roles** come from the record, which says who wants the species from the place, gated by the
literature, which says whether the place has it:

| role | which species | scored on |
|---|---|---|
| primary | the most-wanted species (at least 5 % of the record) that the literature does not contradict or call rare | its band and its size; its characteristic crystal sets the pocket's class |
| alternate | every other such wanted species | its band and its size; any protagonist can carry a pocket's story |
| showpiece | a wanted species the literature calls rare or very rare | the rare band; never carries the story |
| unsupported | a wanted species the literature contradicts (ND, ND?, Q) | listed, not targeted |
| companion | a species the literature calls abundant or common that the record does not want | its band, and not being the star |

Where no source votes (Bimbowrie, the Great Salt Plains, Sulphur Bank), the collectible the sources name
leads, as before. Species the comparison sets aside as outside a scenario's scope get no role.

**Occurrence.** A species occurs in a run when at least one of its crystals survives, at any size. The
bands by literature class:

Specimen lengths under 10 mm are set aside; photographs of one specimen are joined when uploaded within 1,000,000 s (11.6 days) of each other. Display range: the record's q25–q90; a fresh crystal from the record's population lands in it with probability 0.65 × (n − 1) / (n + 1) for a record of n. Story frequency: the largest band floor × that coverage among a scenario's protagonists that can carry a story run — an engine, a band, and a size target whose display range starts within the cavity's span (q25 ≤ span) — crystal targets first, a proxy only where no crystal target can. Noise: the Wilson interval at z 1.96. Clutter: under 10 % of the visible crystals. The star: a wanted species in at least 50 % of the runs.
<!-- /generated:rules -->

No band reaches 100 %. With 21 seeds, a single species in every seed is within noise of a 90–95 %
ceiling, so the scoreboard also tests the fleet as a whole (§2.5).

**Size targets** come from photograph captions, not listings: few listing titles state a size, and those
that do give the specimen.
- *The capture.* The Mineralienatlas locality pages the review already counts were opened in an
  ordinary browser session, page by page. On each loaded page, a read-only extractor recorded the
  caption text around every length (`DATA-STORY-POCKET-SIZES-CAPTURE-2026-10-22.json`). Each page
  payload carries its own hash and the extractor's source hash, and the evidence tool refuses the
  capture unless both recompute.
- *What was read.* For each wanted species: the overview page (up to six photographs per mineral and
  sub-locality), then that species' galleries, largest first, until at least 20 photographs had been
  read or every gallery had.
- *Reading a length.* The evidence tool classes each length as one of:
  - a crystal of the photograph's first-listed mineral ("Kristallgröße", "Kristalle bis", "XX",
    "Kantenlänge", "N mm großer Kristall"; a title naming one crystal, "Gips-Kristall", makes the next
    line's "Größe" that crystal's);
  - a crystal's width or thickness — not a length, so not used;
  - a named aggregate (rosette, sphere, sheaf);
  - the whole specimen ("Größe", "Stufe", the dealer's bare or labelled dimensions);
  - the picture's width ("Bildbreite", "BB");
  - unclassed, where a cut-off caption hides the noun.

  "… hat eine Größe von" takes its subject ("Er", a crystal; "die Stufe", a specimen). A statement
  about the find, "die Kluft enthält bis zu 20 cm lange …", describes the cleft, not the photographed
  specimen, and is dropped.
- *Counting.*
  - A crystal length is set aside when it names another mineral, or names none while its line or title
    is about another mineral. A pseudomorph is the mineral it is made of ("Malachit nach Azurit").
  - A specimen length under 10 mm is set aside: micromount captions give the crystal or the field of
    view as "Größe", and one misread would pull a proxy below the crystals it bounds.
  - Photographs of one specimen count once. They are joined when uploaded together — the image ids read
    as upload times in seconds, and the window is 1,000,000 s, about 11.6 days — with the same caption
    (identical once a word cut at the stretch's start, punctuation and "mit", "und", "auf" are dropped)
    or the same multi-dimension length of a crystal, an aggregate or the specimen; a picture's size joins
    nothing. Uploaded apart, only the same length in three dimensions, one of them written with a decimal
    ("5x5x4,8mm", "15,9 x 10 x 4,7 cm"), joins them. Captions naming
    different finds never join, nor two that each name a mineral the other does not ("mit etwas
    Limonit" against "mit etwas Hämatit": two specimens of one lot).
  - Lengths in one wording once the numbers are taken out, uploaded in one stretch — each less than a
    year after the one before — are one series: likely one contributor's lot or find, not independent
    samples. The capture has no uploader names, so the stretch stands in for the contributor. A
    standard caption field such as "Größe: N mm" is everyone's wording, so only photographs uploaded
    together (within the join window) make a series of it. The same rule applies to specimen lengths,
    and §2.6 re-scores proxies too. A crystal target
    whose largest series holds more than half its lengths is one step less confident, and the scoreboard
    tests the verdict without that series (§2.6).
  - Tiger's eye, chrysoprase, chalcedony and jasper are collected as pieces, so their lengths are piece
    sizes.
- *The target.* A target needs at least five specimens. Crystal lengths come first. Failing that, the
  specimen dimensions serve as a **proxy**. A specimen is at least as long as its own crystals, so a
  proxy overstates the crystal. Reaching it is strong evidence, but not proof: the photographs that give
  crystal lengths and those that give specimen sizes are different photographs, and at Viburnum the
  three photographs with a calcite crystal length are no smaller than the specimens. Falling short of a
  proxy is no evidence. With fewer than five of either, the size is **unknown**.
- *The display range and the size verdict.* A run is at display scale when its crystal lies between the
  record's lower quartile and its 90th percentile. Over the runs where the species occurs, in a cavity
  that holds its median, their median against the record:
  - BELOW (under the lower quartile);
  - MEETS (in the middle half, around the record's median);
  - LARGE (between the upper quartile and the 90th percentile);
  - OVERSIZE (past it).

  Against a proxy the verdict is BELOW PROXY (no verdict), REACHES PROXY (oversize cannot be tested
  against a proxy) or OVERSIZE. A simulated crystal larger than every photographed crystal length of
  its species, where a few exist, is flagged.

**Pockets.** A cavity can hold a species' characteristic crystal when its span — the longest distance
between two wall vertices (`tools/story-pocket-cavities.mjs`) — is at least the target's median. The
span is an upper bound on the longest crystal the cavity holds: in a cavity that is not convex, the
chord can cross the wall. So SMALL is conclusive and STORY-SCALE is generous.
- A pocket is SMALL when it cannot hold the primary protagonist's characteristic crystal. Small pockets
  leave the story count (their stories are reported apart); they never shrink the target.
- A pocket is UNCLASSED when this cannot be told: the primary's size is unknown, or it is a proxy
  longer than the cavity. A run without a story there stays open.
- A crystal longer than the cavity's span cannot be held by the starting cavity. It neither carries the
  story nor counts against it, and it is flagged.

**The story frequency.** A run carries the story when a primary or alternate protagonist is at its
record's display scale. A scenario should carry it in at least *floor × coverage* of its scored runs,
for the protagonist that can carry a story run with the largest such product. A protagonist can carry
one when it has an engine, a band and a size target whose display range starts within the scenario's
cavity span: its lower quartile no longer than the span, the same test a run's YES needs. Crystal
targets come first. A proxy sets the frequency only where no crystal target can carry a story run, as a
size target prefers crystal lengths.
- the floor is its band's lower bound;
- the coverage is the chance that a crystal drawn from its record's population lands in the display
  range of a record its size: 0.65 × (n − 1) / (n + 1), so 0.43 for a record of five crystals and 0.58
  for seventeen, approaching 65 % as the record grows. It is exact for a uniform population and within
  about 0.03 for skewed ones such as collectors' sizes (the tests simulate both).

What the product means depends on the target:
- *For a crystal target* it is the *expected* story rate of a fleet whose protagonist occurs at its
  band's floor with crystals drawn from its record's population. It is not a guarantee: with 21 seeds
  such a fleet falls short about half the time. The verdict marks a shortfall "noise" when the 95 %
  interval of the runs that carry or could carry the story still reaches the frequency. A shortfall
  beyond noise is rare: the tests draw such fleets at every committed sample size, in a cavity that
  holds the whole range and in one that cuts it at the median.
- *For a specimen proxy* it is a bar for STORY (PROXY): the rate at which crystals as large as the
  photographed specimens would reach the proxy's range. It is not an expected rate, since the proxy
  overstates the crystal. Most frequencies today are bars of this kind, and the targets file says which.

What the cavity does, and does not do:
- *It never cuts the coverage.* Runs whose crystal is longer than the cavity stay open, so a cavity
  shorter than the range's top leaves the verdict open rather than failing it. The size target is never
  shrunk to fit.
- *It does decide who can carry a story run.* A protagonist whose display range starts beyond the span
  cannot, so it does not set the frequency. Dropping it can lower the frequency (Elmwood's calcite) or
  remove it (four scenarios). The basis of every frequency names the protagonists dropped, and the
  second line under §2's table lists them all. The size verdict's own eligibility test uses the median instead;
  §2.6 shows the verdicts under that test, and with no cavity test at all.

It asks for no protagonist in every seed, and no size spread narrower than the record's.
- *How it got here.* Review round 3 showed that the floor alone failed a fleet that met every band and
  size target: a protagonist would have had to occur in about 77 % of the seeds. Round 4 showed that a
  fixed 65 % coverage still overstated what a small record's range holds, and that a protagonist with no
  size target could set a frequency it could never carry. Round 5 showed the same of a protagonist whose
  range starts beyond the cavity (Elmwood's calcite), and that the rate is expected only for crystal
  targets. Round 6 showed that the cavity's part had to be stated rather than denied, and that a proxy's
  bar was gating STORY on crystal targets; hence crystal targets first.
- *What it does not check.* The story verdict checks the frequency alone. WP1's whole target also needs
  every protagonist within its band, the protagonist that carries the story at the record's size (its
  typical crystal within the record's middle half, rule 4), and none oversize. The scoreboard reports
  that too, under the WP1 table. With 21 seeds the check is weak: a protagonist in 7 seeds is within
  noise of a 50 % floor, and where the frequency falls below 0.155 even a fleet that never tells the
  story is within noise (the fifth line under §2's table).

**Variety.** The scoreboard reports, per scenario:
- how many distinct assemblages (sets of surviving species) its seeds grow;
- which protagonists occur in every seed;
- how much each protagonist's largest crystal varies across the seeds (interquartile range over median),
  beside the record's.

For the fleet, it gives how many species would occur in every seed if each occurred at its band's
ceiling, and the chance that every species seen in all of its seeds would be.

**The visible size.** "Visible" — at least 1 mm and not flagged as enclosed — is the review's convention,
and the census counts such crystals at exactly 1 mm. Here it marks clutter and the dust and hidden
flags only; it decides no verdict.

**The design numbers that await the boss.** These are proposals, not measurements:
- the band for each literature class, with ceilings below 100 %;
- the 5 % wanted share;
- the five-specimen minimum, with confidence low below 10 and medium below 30;
- the display range (the record's lower quartile to its 90th percentile), and the median-centred size
  verdict. These are this proposal's reading of "use the median observed crystal size as the central
  showcase target, with a range/quantiles rather than a single cutoff";
- eligibility at the median;
- the story-frequency rule (the band floor × the display range's coverage of a record the target's
  size, crystal targets first), the cavity test for setters (the range's start, not the median), and
  the 95 % interval that marks noise;
- the rule for joining photographs of one specimen, with its 11.6-day window;
- the 10 mm floor on specimen lengths, and the series rule (one wording, uploads less than a year
  apart) that lowers a dominant series' confidence;
- WP2's and WP3's targets: the star a wanted species in at least half the runs, clutter under 10 % of
  the visible crystals;
- the caption parser's rules. Every crystal length the parser used, and every join, is listed in
  `DATA-STORY-POCKET-SIZES-2026-10-22.md`.

## 2. Where the scenarios stand: the story-pocket scoreboard

The scoreboard reports, per scenario:
- the cavity, and its class against the primary protagonist's characteristic crystal;
- each role holder's occurrence against its band;
- its size target, with the sample count and quality;
- its largest unflagged crystal when it occurs (median and quartiles);
- the share of eligible runs at the record's display scale;
- how many runs carry the story, against the scenario's frequency;
- the star, and how often it is a wanted species;
- the variety across the seeds;
- the supporting cast, the visible clutter and the flags.

The verdicts:
- **STORY / STORY (PROXY)** — the story frequency is met, on crystal targets / only with proxies.
- **THIN STORY / NO STORY** — it is carried in some runs / in none. "Noise" marks a shortfall that 21
  runs cannot tell from the target.
- **UNRESOLVED** — no story frequency, or runs still open could carry it: a protagonist with no crystal
  target or short of a specimen proxy, a crystal longer than the cavity, or no story in an unclassed
  pocket.
- **SMALL POCKETS ONLY** — no cavity holds the primary's characteristic crystal.
- **NO ENGINE / OUT OF SCOPE** — as before.

Why the verdicts moved:
- **The size bar is now each species' own record.** The old rule's "thumbnail" and "cabinet" were 5 and
  10 mm for every species. Grimsel's quartz passed it and Elmwood's fluorite and calcite were "cabinet".
  Against their photographed crystals or specimens they fall short.
- **Falling short of a proxy is not a verdict.** Where the captions give specimen dimensions but too few
  crystal lengths, a simulated crystal below the proxy may still be at the record's crystal scale. That
  is why Elmwood, Jeffrey Mine and most former PARTIAL scenarios are UNRESOLVED, not NO STORY.
- **No default size and no size floor.** The Great Salt Plains' selenite, the old rule's clearest
  showcase, has two photographs with a stated length, so its size is unknown. A protagonist grown as
  dust is flagged, but against an unknown size or a proxy it is still open: Ametista's quartz,
  Cruzeiro's tourmaline and Schneeberg's erythrite.
- **Unclassed pockets stay open — a rule that decides nothing yet.** In every unclassed scenario the runs
  are already open for another reason (the breakdown under the table).
- **One specimen counts once.** Ouro Preto's topaz target rested on one crystal photographed twice under
  different captions. Joined, it has four crystal lengths, too few for a target, so its proxy decides and
  the verdict is UNRESOLVED.
- **A misread caption, a floor, and a frequency.** Review round 2 found Tsumeb's malachite proxy holding a
  pseudomorph crystal at its lower quartile, filed as a specimen, with micromount lengths below it. The
  caption fix alone left the verdict standing; the 10 mm floor under specimen lengths is what moved it to
  UNRESOLVED (review round 3 traced this; round 2's response had credited the caption). Under the story
  frequency revised in round 3 it is STORY (PROXY) again, and §2.6 shows the rules it turns on.
- **One protagonist can carry the story.** The old rule failed the North Pennines vein twice: its quartz
  never forms, and its star was calcite. Now the missing quartz is a flag against its band, the calcite
  star is reported, and the vein's fluorite tells the story.

**Why first.** These are the stories the scenarios exist to tell. Herkimer's quartz ("Herkimer
diamonds"), Cruzeiro's tourmaline and Ametista's amethyst each hold most of their locality's record (the
targets file's record shares).

**The science.** The sources say the crystals are large where the story is:
- "Pockets in this region frequently contain superb gem tourmaline crystals" at Cruzeiro (Proctor 1985).
- Large amethyst lines the Ametista geodes (Proust & Fontaine 2007).
- Selenite grows to 11 m at Naica (García-Ruiz et al. 2007).
- Chiastolitic andalusite reaches 14 cm at Bimbowrie (Teale 1979).
- Calcite scalenohedra reach 2–3 ft at Picher (Ransome 1935).

In nature, crystals grow large where few nuclei share a supply for a long time, close to equilibrium,
and Naica's are the extreme case. In the simulator, dust means one of three things in a given
scenario:
- the species' growth window is too short;
- its supersaturation stays near the nucleation threshold;
- too many nuclei share the supply.

**The lever.** Diagnose each scenario's growth window first: the species' σ history, the event that
feeds it, and its number of nuclei. Then change the scenario (broth, event timing) before any engine
rate. Quartz grows in 19 scenarios, so a global quartz rate would move all of them.

**Target (pre-registered in the targets file; it replaces the 5 mm rule).**
- In at least the scenario's story frequency of its scored runs (story-scale and unclassed pockets;
  small pockets are reported apart), a primary or alternate protagonist is at its record's display
  scale.
- Each protagonist with an engine occurs within its band (a miss that 21 seeds cannot tell from the band
  counts as within).
- Each protagonist that carries story runs is at the record's size: its typical crystal lies within the
  record's middle half (MEETS, rule 4), or reaches its proxy where only a proxy exists. Too small fails as
  too large does; a carrier short of its proxy is open, since a proxy cannot refute.
- None is OVERSIZE.

The story verdict checks the first bullet alone; the line under the WP1 table reports which scenarios
meet all four. A target changes only by a reviewed rewrite of its rules or its evidence, never to fit an
output.

**Cases that need a decision first.**
- **Naica.** The scenario declares "the sim isn't trying to reproduce Naica scale, it's reproducing

  crystal lengths, from a few centimetres to over twenty, against specimens over a decimetre (§2.4).
  Two decisions:
  - should the selenite dominate the pocket at the scale of its record;
  - should the giant-crystal cave be a pocket class of its own (WP9)?
- **Habit, not size.**
  - Tiger's eye and crocidolite at the Asbestos Hills are fibrous seams, not single crystals. Their

### WP8 — Model the mechanisms that make the supporting cast

Why are minerals no collector names so common in real pockets? The boss offered two theories
(2026-10-21):

> "i think some crystals are compatable, so that it reads as a valid build surface when the host rock
> might not, like chemically or mechanically the two bond better so that allows the growth of the seed.
> my other theory is that when certain chemistry is taken out it leaves the rest of the broth to be
> concentrated with other minerals"

A friend's Herkimer specimen the boss showed makes the point. It has one clear quartz "diamond" among
cloudier quartz and possibly calcite crystals, on a carpet of tan dolomite rhombs over dark dolostone,
with black patches that look like anthraxolite. Nothing of note fluoresces under UV. The simulator's
Herkimer pocket grows one sub-millimetre quartz crystal and nothing else, in every seed (the census; the WP1 table gives its size).

**(a) Build surfaces.**

*The science.* A crystal nucleates more easily on a surface that lowers the energy cost of the new
interface (heterogeneous nucleation; De Yoreo & Vekilov 2003). The closer the atomic match, the lower
the cost, down to oriented overgrowth (epitaxy). The closest match of all is the same mineral, on which a
crystal can grow without nucleating at all. The simulator's own substrate table rests on this: "heterogeneous
nucleation reduces the interfacial-free-energy barrier compared to bare-wall nucleation"
(`js/26-mineral-paragenesis.ts`, with Ramdohr 1980 for the sulfide pairs).

*Where the simulator stands.* `tools/substrate-census.mjs` classifies where every crystal ever nucleated
started, across all 41 scenarios at seed 42:

Of the starts on another species, 84 (25 %) were pairs in the simulator's substrate table; the rest are placements the engines write themselves.

The table holds 58 host → species pairs on 19 host minerals. This census used 16 of them (sphalerite → barite 36, sphalerite → galena 11, galena → barite 6, chalcopyrite → chalcocite 5, quartz → feldspar 5, …). None is a same-mineral pair, and none involves dolomite.

Uravan's two unclassed starts are a reducing site, not a surface.

A "composition" can be a physics proxy for the dissolution chemistry rather than the true host; the render matrix, where one is set, is the true host.

Where the wall's own mineral does grow, it starts on the bare wall every time, at the bare-wall threshold:
the wall gives its own mineral no head start.

This bears on a decision the July review defended: dolomite's absence as correct kinetics, because
dolomite is notoriously hard to *nucleate* at low temperature (the locality review's §5.1 records the defence and
the abundance evidence against it). Growth on dolomite that already exists does not need a new nucleus.
That may be how dolomite rhombs form in a dolostone-walled pocket like Herkimer's. A wall-rock surface
would let the simulator test this without overturning the kinetic argument where there is no dolomite to
grow on.

*The lever.* Three additions, each with a source. Each is a SIM bump that moves every carbonate-walled
scenario, so each takes the two-commit pattern and a full census:
1. same-mineral entries — the strongest tier, which the table lacks; a roadmap note flagged this
   "missing diagonal" in July;
2. the wall's own rock as a surface for its own minerals — a dolostone wall for dolomite, a limestone
   wall for calcite, a sandstone wall for quartz;
3. documented pairings for the supporting-cast minerals, dolomite first.

*Target.* Where a scenario's sources name the wall mineral as a pocket mineral (Herkimer's dolomite
rhombs), it forms as the lining the showcase sits on, not as the star.

**(b) Residual concentration.**

*The science.* It works in two ways.
- *Ratios.* Precipitating calcite removes calcium and carbonate and leaves magnesium behind, raising the
  Mg/Ca ratio that favours dolomite.
- *Volume.* When crystals take up most of the dissolved load, whatever does not fit them concentrates in
  the last fluid. In granitic pegmatites this is how pockets come to hold tourmaline, beryl, lepidolite
  and rare phosphates (London 2008). The arrojadite-group phosphates the boss named are themselves
  products of it. They occur in the most evolved, late-stage pegmatite bodies (Birch 2018, Mount
  Wills), their iron-manganese balance tracks how far fractionation went (Keller, Namibian pegmatites),
  and later fluids alter them to other phosphates (Vignola & Diella 2007, Piona). These three are known
  from search summaries only, not read first-hand.

*Where the simulator stands.*
- Every crystal's growth draws the fluid down: the engines subtract what each crystal takes, and fluid
  mass balance has been on since v19. So the ratio form happens.
- The volume form exists only as evaporation: the drying mechanic that Searles uses multiplies
  `fluid.concentration` (`js/85c-simulator-state.ts`).
- As the pocket fills, the leftover fluid is not concentrated. The fill level only seals the cavity and
  damps nucleation.

*The hypothesis to test first.* Cruzeiro's tourmaline is dust (the WP1 table), though it holds most of the record, while
feldspar is the largest crystal in every seed. If boron never concentrates in the late fluid, the
tourmaline is starved.

*The lever.* A residual-concentration term: as crystals take up the pocket, the remaining fluid's
leftover components rise. Try it on `gem_pegmatite` against WP1's target before any other scenario.

### WP9 — Give each scenario a distribution: pockets, occurrence and size

**Problem.**
- Every scenario grows in one cavity in all its seeds (§2.3), most of them about 50 mm across, whatever
  the locality. A scenario's fleet therefore holds one pocket class, never small pockets and story-scale
  ones together.
- Across its seeds a scenario grows the same species at nearly the same sizes (§2.5). The species
  present in every seed do not come out of noise.
- The contract asks for distributions of all three (rules 3 and 4): small pockets that may lack a
  showcase alongside pockets that tell the story at the record's scale, signature minerals common but
  not guaranteed, and crystal sizes that vary as the record's do.

**Where the lever is.**
- *The pocket.* The cavity comes from the scenario's authored wall: its `vug_diameter_mm` or
  `size_class`, and its `shape_seed`. `js/22-geometry-wall.ts` can already draw a diameter within a
  size class, given a random draw (`_size_rng`, which Random Mode uses). A per-seed draw within a range
  each scenario authors would give every scenario a distribution of pockets.
- *Occurrence and size.* What a scenario grows is decided by its broth and its events, which are the
  same in every seed. A per-seed variation within the ranges the sources give — the fluid's
  composition, an event's timing or strength — would let a protagonist be common rather than
  guaranteed, and its size vary.
- Either moves every scenario: a SIM bump, the two-commit pattern and a full census.

**The decisions it needs.**
- Each scenario's ranges. For a locality whose record shows crystals longer than its cavity — a proxy
  for now, crystal lengths after WP0 — the pocket range must reach them.
- Naica's case: whether the giant-crystal cave is its own pocket class. The wall module's "cave" class
  spans 0.3–3 m.
- Which inputs may vary per seed, and by how much: a choice about the science of each deposit, not a
  tuning knob.

**Target.**
- Each scenario's fleet holds story-scale pockets for its primary's characteristic crystal, and the
  share of small pockets is reported, not hidden.
- No locality's target is shrunk to fit the cavity.
- Each protagonist occurs within its band without being in every seed, and the size spread across the
  seeds approaches the record's.

## 5. Limits

- **The record is a mixture.** It mixes abundance, collector grade, desire and market noise. So it
  chooses which species to feature and at what scale, never how often they occur or how many crystals a
  pocket holds.
- **Photographed sizes are collectors' sizes.** People photograph their better specimens. The targets
  are display scale by design — the story pocket's, not the average pocket's.
- **Crystal lengths are scarce.** Most targets are specimen proxies or unknown (§2.4). A proxy overstates
  the crystal: reaching it is strong evidence, falling short of it none. Because crystal lengths and
  specimen sizes come from different photographs, even reaching a proxy is not proof.
- **The capture is a sample.** For most species it stops at about 20 photographs, following the rule in
  §1.3. Where an archive holds more photographs, the rest are unread (WP0).
- **The caption parser is heuristic.**
  - It reads German phrasing, cut-off captions, the nearest cue, the noun's gender and the title.
  - Review round 1 hand-checked all 194 crystal and aggregate lengths it then used and 80 of its specimen
    lengths: about 1–2 % and 2.5 % were wrong. Round 2 read every crystal row again and a seeded sample
    of 60 specimen lengths behind the proxies: 2 of the 60 (3 %) were wrong. Round 4 sampled 70 more: 1
    (1.5 %). Rounds 3–5 read every crystal row behind the targets again and found them clean. The cases
    the rounds found are fixed and are now tests.
  - A diameter after a number is read as a width, except after a tabular or rounded form ("tafelige
    Kristalle", "Eisenrosen", a rosette cut to "…ette"), whose diameter is its size. A stretch cut before
    any such word ("n cyanblauer Farbe und bis zu 11 mm Durchmesser", a cavansite) keeps the width
    reading; another wording that gives a length as a diameter would also be lost from the lengths.
  - A size in three dimensions is never a picture's width, whatever word follows it ("40x35x20 mm
    Ausschnitt" is a detail of a 40 mm specimen).
  - Every crystal length it uses, and every join, is listed in `DATA-STORY-POCKET-SIZES-2026-10-22.md`.
  - Specimen lengths under 10 mm are set aside (§1.3): on a micromount the caption's "Größe" usually
    gives the crystal or the field. A real specimen that small is set aside with them.
- **One specimen, one sample — by a heuristic.** Photographs are joined when they were uploaded
  together and their captions are the same (once a cut word, punctuation and "mit", "und", "auf" are
  dropped) or state the same multi-dimension size of a crystal, an aggregate or the specimen (a
  picture's size joins nothing); uploaded apart, only a
  three-dimension size written with a decimal joins them. Never across two named finds, or two captions
  that each name a mineral the other does not (§1.3).
  - "Uploaded together" reads the image id as an upload time in seconds. That is inferred from the ids'
    range, which runs from 2004 to just before the capture; the site does not document it.
  - Two specimens with an identical caption uploaded the same week would be joined. A re-photographed
    specimen with a new caption, uploaded apart and with no measured size, would not.
- **One contributor can make most of a sample.** A run of captions in one wording ("Fluorit-XX max. N
  mm"), uploaded within months, is likely one collector's lot or one find; a standard caption field
  ("Größe: N mm") counts as a series only when uploaded together. Where one such series holds
  more than half of a crystal target's lengths, the target's confidence drops a step, and §2.6 re-scores
  the verdict without it. The capture has no uploader names, so the series is a proxy for the
  contributor: two contributors who share a wording in the same year would be one series, and one who
  changes wording would be two.
- **First-listed mineral.** A crystal length that names no mineral is credited to the photograph's
  first-listed mineral unless its line or title is about another. In a photograph of several minerals
  this can still misattribute it.
- **Small samples.** A crystal target from five specimens is low confidence, and §2.6 shows that the
  minimum sample moves more verdicts than any other rule. With 21 runs, an occurrence rate is uncertain
  by about ±20 points, and the scoreboard marks such shortfalls "noise". The story check is weak for the
  same reason. A fleet at exactly its expected story rate falls short about half the time, and a
  protagonist in 7 of 21 seeds is within noise of a 50 % floor. More seeds per scenario would sharpen
  both.
- **The bands are a design mapping** from the literature's classes, not measurements.
- **One cavity per scenario, as it starts.** The census does not keep the final wall, so a cavity widened
  by dissolution is not seen. Its span is the longest chord between two wall vertices, not checked to
  run inside the cavity: an upper bound on the crystal it can hold. A SMALL class is therefore
  conclusive and a STORY-SCALE class generous.
- **The census is end-state.** It does not see a crystal that grew large and then dissolved.
- **Thin records make weak targets.** At Bingham, Sulphur Bank, Bimbowrie and the Great Salt Plains the
  literature carries the story, and the photographs give little or no size.
